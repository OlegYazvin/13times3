## Atmosphere and setting

Oleg wants inspiration from photographs of the real Shedd Aquarium without a literal reconstruction. Aim for a majestic, coherent 3D storybook aquarium with recognizable architectural cues and a family-centered frozen moment. The fish can drift or turn slightly in a restrained loop while the people and main moment remain still. Do not make the entire tableau a static flat image.

On 2026-10-21, Oleg chose **G and Zephrine wondering at the fish together** as the central moment. Give G a clear, affectionate role beside Zephrine. Keep Jane and Oleg present only as the composition supports the family memory; do not invent a named exhibit they visited.

The room uses [Shedd's history of the building](https://www.sheddaquarium.org/about-shedd/vision/history) and [Wonder of Water in its historic rotunda](https://www.sheddaquarium.org/exhibits/wonder-of-water) for visual vocabulary: pale marble, a water-themed glass dome, and tall habitats. This is a symbolic aquarium, not a claim that the family saw a particular exhibit. The visit date was not supplied.

## Portal topology and movement requirement

The interior marble return arch is freestanding, with a walkable loop behind either post. The actual hall boundary is 1.85 meters farther back, so Jane can circle the posts while remaining inside the aquarium; only crossing the centered gallery aperture exits. This retains a single world camera, direct parallax, and continuous position and field of view. The shallow area behind the arch is the physical compromise imposed by placing a room behind an existing hallway wall. Test the loop, aperture, collision, and visual treatment from inside before accepting it.

Painted deep-water side panoramas, small fish, and sea fans keep the gallery's plaster out of the view from behind the arch. The loop and posts were checked from both sides in browser emulation.
