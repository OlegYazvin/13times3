# Lessons for future gallery scenes

These are decisions and corrections from developing frame `01`. Read them with the [scene registry](scene-registry.md) before building another room. Apply the interaction lessons to every room; choose each new room's own visual theme from its memory.

## Art direction

- Make the hallway spacious, dreamlike, and architectural. Avoid a narrow digital tunnel, repeated hexagons, empty flat walls, and decorative elements that hide labels or signs.
- Blank frames keep their two-digit number plaques. Every completed room needs a short `room.caption` in the gallery manifest; display it as a plaque across the painting's upper edge in place of that number. Keep it readable from the center of the wider hallway and clear of nearby trim and pilasters. A plaque mounted above the tall frame falls outside the desktop camera's normal field of view.
- Make each room a coherent, detailed 3D storybook tableau. Give surfaces, ceiling, props, figures, and lighting a shared visual language. Use custom contours, layered materials, carvings, and intentional silhouettes; large default spheres, ovals, and egg shapes make the scene look unfinished.
- Treat boxes, cylinders, spheres, cones, planes, and low-poly ovals as hidden construction tools, not finished visible assets. At normal walking distance, a person, desk, tree, building, vehicle, or story prop must read as that specific thing from its silhouette and modeled details without relying on its object name or a caption. Sculpt contours, joints, folds, openings, faces, and material transitions; remove decorative geometry that reads as unexplained polygons. More polygons alone do not equal more detail. Reject a room that looks like a collection of primitives even if the scene graph is large.
- Stage and furniture silhouettes matter even in a small portal painting: frame `01`'s simple rectangular desk and platform looked digital despite sea ornaments. Use shaped, beveled surfaces, a readable carved apron and turned legs, and continuous trim; retain a collision footprint for each large prop. Small trim cannot rescue an otherwise block-shaped story object.
- Match recurring people to the private reference photos before publishing. Check the face shape and distinctive features, hair, glasses or jewelry, posture, and clothing in both the doorway view and close three-quarter views. In frame `02`, Jane needs her dark high bun, distinctive brows and face, and Oleg needs his side-parted longer dark hair, rectangular glasses, and short beard. Painted likeness detail can be mapped onto a genuinely sculpted head, but it must not read as a flat face pasted onto a blocky figure. Keep every figure in the room's shared illustrated style.
- Reuse the shared illustrated Oleg portrait on each sculpted Oleg head. A generic pair of eye spheres and a beard curve are not an acceptable likeness. Check the face at normal walking distance, not only enlarged in the author preview; also keep recognizable side hair, glasses, and beard in the three-quarter silhouette. Add material, seam, joint, and object-specific detail to every prominent figure and prop. A higher object count alone does not make a scene more legible.
- Keep Oleg's modeled hair subordinate to the portrait. Four thick swept locks per side turned into dark bars that covered his forehead, glasses, and ear in room views; a continuous side/back shell and one modest nape curl preserve the longer side-parted silhouette without obscuring the reference-guided face.
- Keep portrait texture and sculpted head proportions together. A square painted face stretched over a tall cranium makes Oleg look unlike himself; a large head can also hide a child or story prop behind it. Inspect the face, hair, and any person on his shoulders from the doorway and a close three-quarter angle. If resizing a figure's head, check every nearby person's face and pose again before release.
- Use suitable lightweight, license-compatible prebuilt 3D models when hand-authored procedural geometry cannot reach the intended finish. Adapt their proportions, materials, colors, and detail so they belong in the room; keep their license and attribution in the source notes. Inspect them from all walkable angles and test mobile size/performance. Do not ship a recognizable raw primitive as a stand-in merely to avoid sourcing or authoring a proper model.
- Check a candidate model in the actual narrative pose before replacing a figure. The [Quaternius CC0 animated man](https://poly.pizza/m/HMnuH5geEG) has better adult proportions but its raw facets still read low-poly. The [Innerscene CC0 static adult](https://www.innerscene.com/tools/library/3d-parts/abstract-white-man-3d-person-standing-9e06c5d7) keeps its silhouette after simplification, but its fixed hanging arms cannot show Oleg holding a page, pushing Zephrine's car, or carrying her. A body swap that breaks these actions is a regression; either rig and adapt a suitable model or continue sculpting pose-specific geometry. Neither candidate is included in the published rooms yet.
- The adapted [CC0 clothed Sam rig](rigged-figure-source.md) now supplies Oleg's body in frames `02`, `04`, `05`, `07`, `08`, `09`, `10`, and `11`. Keep its fitted clothes and hands in the same painted palette as the room; remove the source's unrelated face and props. Pose each instance for the actual action, especially touching a page, reaching the push-car grip, or supporting Zephrine on his shoulders. A neutral A-pose pasted into each world would repeat the old problem in a smoother mesh.
- Every character and important prop should have volume from multiple viewpoints. Do not use 2D photo cutouts. A recognizable painted likeness does not need photorealism. Keep photos as private references; do not publish the originals or their metadata.
- Jane's, Zephrine's, and Oleg's uploaded reference photos are stored locally under `.secrets/reference-photos/` and excluded from Git. Use them when a later scene includes those people. Oleg's files are `oleg-portrait.jpg` and `oleg-with-jane-and-zephrine.jpg`; they show his medium-length dark brown hair, dark rectangular glasses, and short beard. The private copies have been resized and stripped of EXIF metadata. Do not publish the original photos or these reference copies.
- Anna Shersher is Jane's mother. `anna-and-igor.jpg` shows Anna beside Igor and can guide both of their likenesses; `anna-video-call.jpg` is a second reference for Anna, including her glasses. These files are also private, resized copies with EXIF metadata removed. Do not publish them.
- For Jane in frame `01`, retain her recognizable face and give her stylized body a subtly slimmer silhouette, as requested. Do not treat that as a rule for other people or future scenes.
- Let actions and objects carry the story. Frame `01` does not need “acting” and “dance” signs behind Zephrine; the stage and signup details imply them. Avoid invented studios, dates, enrollment facts, or other biographical claims.
- Frame `01` has a mermaid and undersea cave theme because Zephrine likes *The Little Mermaid*. Its curved cavern shell, sea plants, shells, and coral should feel authored rather than assembled from primitives. Do not impose this theme on unrelated memories.

## Portal and movement behavior

- Keep the painting opaque at a distance and preload only its room model nearby. Reveal the real room through irregular openings in the painting as Jane approaches. Render the room behind an actual aperture in the gallery wall, with parallax from the same world camera. Do not use a periodically refreshed texture, screen fade, or camera teleport.
- Crossing the aperture in either direction preserves world position, view direction, and field of view. The return doorway shows the actual hallway, not a still capture. Keep the nearby room loaded on exit and release it after Jane walks away.
- Collide with walls, stage, desk, and figures. Walking through a character or other solid object breaks the scene. Keep the return opening obvious and traversable while the rest of the boundary is solid.
- Advance each movement substep from the last accepted position, including when a large elapsed frame or test move spans a narrow post. A rejected substep must never be followed by a candidate placed beyond that obstacle; test long moves against thin architecture and people as well as ordinary walking speed.
- Each portal leads to an independent render space. Neighboring rooms may occupy the same coordinates behind adjacent frames; stencil the active world to its doorway so its geometry cannot appear in the hall or another world. Keep only one room loaded, preserve the single camera and its pose while crossing, and apply collision only to the active room. Test adjoining frames and turns from oblique hall and interior views to catch leaking geometry.
- Treat portal adjacency as a visual composition problem, never as a reason to shrink a room or cap its sky. Keep the room model in its own scene and draw it only through its assigned stencil opening. On exit, unload that world's model after moving away; loading its neighbor must leave exactly one room world active. A doorway's physical position in the hallway does not constrain the size of the world behind it.
- A room can place its actual gallery aperture inside a larger portal world. Store `room.portalZ` separately from half the world depth, align the room model to that portal plane, and leave walkable space behind the freestanding doorway. Crossing its active front face changes worlds; walking around its posts or behind it stays in the room. From behind, render the room through the arch instead of showing a misleading second exit. Test the path around both posts and the actual exit plane.
- Populate habitats and landscapes in volume. A detailed painted backdrop may give a tank depth, but visible fish, plants, glass, rock, and framing must occupy distinct 3D layers. Keep moving creatures inside their habitats and behind collision boundaries. At the normal doorway distance, their silhouette and story action should still read.
- On phones, the left thumb joystick moves. A drag begun anywhere else on the scene looks around; holding the displaced finger left or right continuously turns in that direction, without repeated swipes. Drag vertically to look up or down. Preserve desktop keyboard and drag controls.

## Scale, cost, and checks

- Use the current Eleventy, Three.js, GitHub Pages, and Cloudflare setup. Keep rooms static-hosted with no backend or paid service. Load one room at a time, and aim for roughly 8 MB or less of downloaded assets per room.
- A room may be around 10 feet on each side or larger when its scene needs space. Store its dimensions, entry pose, preview camera, model, and preview in `src/_data/gallery.json`.
- Render the portrait preview from the same 3D scene and a hallway viewing pose, so it predicts the room revealed through the frame. Compare screenshots from the far hallway, partial reveal, open frame, entered room, return doorway, and after exit.
- Use the same light colors, intensities, positions, output color space, and camera settings for a room's still painting and its live renderer. A brighter author-only light rig makes the painted scene collapse to a darker one at the portal even when the geometry and camera are continuous. Keep the light rig in one shared source module and check the still against the loaded room at the same pose.
- Verify collision, resource cleanup, the WebGL fallback, resize, sustained phone turning, phone movement, and desktop movement. Preserve all 39 achievement URLs and 41 unique frame IDs. Use phone emulation for development, then check feel and performance on a physical iPhone when available; the target is a steady 30 fps there.
- Inspect screenshots for hidden signs, flat walls, obvious primitive shapes, clipping, mismatched views, and jarring mixed visual styles before publishing.
- Include one screenshot at the normal painting viewing distance and close views of each key character and story prop. If a viewer cannot identify who is who and what each group is doing without reading a label, revise the scene's composition and models before release.
- For long story routes, stand midway through the room and turn to each side. A doorway screenshot can hide a blank or intersecting wall deeper inside. Keep route labels visible from the main path and confirm the panorama still holds up near its far end.
- On a long outdoor route, suspend nothing from an imaginary ceiling. Floating map panes and free polygon fragments made frame `08` look like stage rigging and were removed. Ground the rhythm in the street: built lamps, recognizable facades, paving, and visible labels. A far skyline should have distinct roof forms and windows, not repeated cubes.
- A miniature landmark on the side of a walkable promenade needs an authored side facing the visitor. Frame `08` originally put the detailed front of several stops toward the entrance camera; at each stop, the actual walker saw only a blank box wall. Add dimensional side entrances, columns, arches, and material courses, and angle each label so it remains legible both down the route and from the stop. Check the path-facing close view before calling the architecture detailed.
- For outdoor rooms, make the world feel open: use a continuous sky dome with a graduated horizon and distant terrain rather than visible rectangular sky-wall seams or a low roof. The current domes are about 72 m in radius and their distant terrain spans 144 m, well beyond the authored walkable region, because portal worlds are independently rendered. The preview camera must have the same far plane as the live camera so it actually shows that dome. Check from the hall, room center, and edges on both wide and portrait screens. Keep reachable ground and collision bounds aligned; a larger visible sky alone does not authorize walking into unmodeled terrain.
- On a tall portrait phone, an open sky can occupy most of the image. Give the dome varied, softly painted high cloud and warm pigment passages while leaving its top seam quiet. Compare the park, orchard, and street previews together after changing their shared sky texture so one room does not gain a visible ceiling band or repeated wallpaper stripe.
- The first high-sky pass drew long cloud strokes across the dome's UV map. From inside frame `08`, those turned into repeated arcs and looked like a low ceiling even though the dome was physically tall. Use small, localized pigment blooms and verify from an entered-room camera, not only the doorway preview.
- A broad blank sky can make an otherwise open world look unfinished. The shared dome may carry a restrained, localized cloud wash, but check all seven outdoor paintings after increasing its contrast: do not let cloud marks form a repeated roof seam. The model's collision edge remains grounded at the authored path while the sky and distant terrain extend far beyond it.
- In recurring portraits, put one consistent illustrated face on a curved modeled head, then add only the needed 3D relief. Do not layer each room's old hair ropes or painted glasses over the shared sculpt; duplicates make the face look like a mask or helmet. Inspect the person at actual walking distance after every shared likeness revision, not just the isolated texture.
- Give shoulders, elbows, sleeves, wrists, palms, and thumbs one connected silhouette. A pair of simple cylinders for each arm reads as a jointed toy even when the face is recognizable. Scale the whole painted head sculpture about its jaw, including the hair and glasses, when adjusting proportions; shrinking only the texture or adding unscaled room-specific hair creates a floating mask. Check every reused figure and any child close beside it after a shared change.
- Before exporting any new figure, check that the loft rings rise in order from shoe to shoulder. Increasing leg length while retaining old absolute chest positions can fold the torso through itself. Adult figures should have a leg length near half their height, correctly sized shoes, and a head that carries the same sculpt and painted likeness. Give child figures their own torso ring positions. Thin garment seams, woven pigment, hands with separate fingers, and pose-specific folds add detail that a higher polygon count by itself cannot provide.
- Do not squeeze a reference-based head so far vertically that its features disappear into the shirt. Scale the painted facial surface, modeled hair, glasses, and beard together about the jaw, then compare at doorway scale and close range against the private reference. A better face texture alone cannot fix a compressed toy-sized head or an oversized blocky torso.
- When replacing a recurring person's body with a rigged model, check the head-to-neck join, shoulders, hands, garment fit and collision in the exported GLB, not only in the author preview. Keep the source model compact and document its license. Rebuild every scene containing that person, then compare the figure at its story position in each room; a pose or placement suitable for one memory may break another.
- G appears in the blueberry field and Shedd aquarium. Reuse her adapted posed clothed figure and painted likeness in both; she is Oleg's mother, distinct from Jane's mother Anna. Sculpt the short bob as a continuous volume behind the curved face so its temples do not reveal an abrupt hair-shell edge. Keep blouse seams and collar details small enough that they do not cover the chin or turn into raised blocks at walking distance. Inspect both her front and a three-quarter view beside Zephrine before release.
- A uniformly dark side-hair shell can make a good painted face look like a mask inside a helmet. Oleg's rigged head now has a full sculpted underpainting with skin at the jaw and graduated hair color on the crown and back; the face painting sits over that volume. Keep the top rounded and close to the actual hairline, and inspect it from the same close position in frame `02` after any change. Small garment details should come from reference, such as the wordless sun-and-cloud shirt sketch, not arbitrary symbols.
- For recurring Oleg appearances, keep one active illustrated likeness across all eight GLBs. The newer painted face is closer to his photo in glasses, eyes, hair part and short beard; the old head mapping was retained after a taller UV trial visibly stretched the features. Check that generated face changes improve the character in the actual walkable room, not only as a square texture.
- A faithful portrait texture can still shrink into an anonymous mask if much of its UV space is hair or transparent margin. Compare a fixed close camera in frame `02` before and after cropping the mapping, and retain the same crop across all Oleg appearances. The source photograph remains private. The side and back of the sculpt must also look like layered hair rather than a uniformly dark helmet; reduce cheek coverage, vary the painted hair, and inspect three-quarter views before calling the likeness done.
- A painted face can guide more specific relief without publishing the original photo. Oleg's public illustrated portrait supplies a tiny offline face-landmark depth table for his curved sculpt; keep its influence restrained and rebuild all eight appearances together. A stronger trial made his nose too prominent in a close room view.
- Reject a generic rig that improves anatomy but has the wrong clothes or makes a key action impossible. The raw [CC0 Maya walk rig](https://www.innerscene.com/tools/library/3d-parts/relaxed-walk-maya-3d-person-animated-2c6669a2) has a sleeveless top that did not fit Jane's office, family and outdoor memories. Adapt its garment and pose before using it in a story.
- Standing Jane figures in frames `02`, `05`, `07`, `08`, `10`, and `11` now use that adapted CC0 Maya rig, with full painted sleeves, posed hands, a clothed body, Jane's illustrated face, and a sculpted high bun. Keep the distinctive gestures and room-specific clothing colors; never revert to the raw sleeveless source. Frame `06` keeps its custom seated sculpture. Check the body and head together at doorway scale before extending the rig to other custom authors.
- Keep outdoor branches attached to real trunks. Thin arcs and sparse polygon leaves suspended across the walking path read as rigging and make the sky feel like a stage ceiling. The blue-car street, blueberry field, and apple orchard are more open without them; add canopy only where connected modeled foliage can support it.
- Give the sculpted figures a soft ground contact in every authoring path so their feet do not appear to float. A transparent floor shadow is acceptable; a flat image of a person is not. Recheck that the shadow does not affect collision or make the room asset exceed its mobile budget.
- In an aquarium, draw fish at a size visible from the visitor path and show the glazing, ledge, plants, rock and framing that locate them behind glass. Tiny fish scattered over a backdrop can read as marks on wallpaper. Use nearby volumetric fish and a clear curved tank edge for the first glance, then smaller schools and habitat detail deeper inside.
- When a habitat has several glass bays, vary its painted distant views. A high-detail mural repeated unchanged in every bay still reads as wallpaper. Keep original generated vistas behind actual 3D species, plants, rocks, and glazing; the mural is only a far layer, never a cutout character or substitute for the inhabited tank.
- Put any jellyfish, sea fan or similar flourish physically inside its habitat, with a readable three-dimensional bell, branches and filaments from close and angled views. Preserve translucent materials when batching animated figures for export. Remove isolated ceiling ornaments that look like loose polygons instead of architecture or creatures.
- When a doorway stands free in an expansive room, design what the visitor finds behind it as intentionally as the first frontal view. Use actual exhibits inside the walk-around space and keep the loop around the portal clear after adding their collision footprints. Thin disconnected floor doodles do not substitute for an environment.
- At the far end of a walkable room, keep painted habitat backdrops well behind reachable glazing and put 3D inhabitants between the visitor and the painting. A low-resolution backdrop placed directly on the collision boundary becomes visibly pixelated when approached, even when it looks rich from the doorway. Inspect the rear at actual player eye height.
- Carry designed architecture through every side of a freestanding portal. A luminous dome on the front half of an aquarium still leaves a flat sky box over the walkable area behind its posts unless the rear vault has its own surface. Inspect the view immediately after rounding each post and from the rear glass.
- Species should have different continuous silhouettes: for an aquarium, a turtle needs a domed scute shell, beak and broad paddles; a seahorse needs its upright neck, muzzle and coiled tail. Large generic cones used as coral read as planters. Use branching stems and thick uneven plate growth instead, and keep the detailed painted habitat behind real glass and modeled foreground inhabitants.
- Fish in overhead habitat need a visible enclosing glass edge and lower pane; scattering them against the painted dome reads as floating decoration. Vary their bodies (deep reef fish, streamlined fish, slender fish) as well as palette, and check them from both the entry view and directly underneath. A very large ray seen head-on may turn into an oval; move it to a side habitat or angle it until the swept wings read. Do not add flat floor symbols just to increase detail: the attempted repeated shell fans read like tiny polygons at walking distance and were removed.
- A large architectural support needs its own silhouette and material story, not a faceted shaft with five decorative lines. Carve the flute into the actual column radius, shape its base and capital, and keep its collision footprint. A repeated capital should be restrained enough to read as stonework from the doorway. Strong random water marks across a broad floor look like stains; use fine stone grain and quiet caustics, then inspect the floor at human eye height.
- The CC0 static toddler candidate [Mina](https://www.innerscene.com/tools/library/3d-parts/mina-3d-person-toddler-standing-a3361460) was inspected for Zephrine but has no skin or armature. Do not replace a posed Zephrine on Oleg's shoulders, pointing at fish, or steering her car with an inert standing model. A child-body adaptation needs to preserve each action and her painted likeness before it is published.
- The shared child skirt in frames `05`, `06`, `07`, `10`, and `11` now follows a fitted waist through cloth pleats to a narrower fluted hem. Check its length and its relation to leggings and shoes from both the portal view and phone close-up. The earlier broad three-ring cone read like an egg; simply shrinking it into a short flat apron also looked wrong. The child head's hair volume has a lower crown aligned to the face painting. This is still a stylized sculpt, so its torso, hands, and side view need further art review.
- Do not scale adult garment seam drawings down onto a child. In the blueberry and aquarium close views, the chest and side lines became detached-looking loops around Zephrine; a short collar and the already modeled skirt hem read more cleanly. For a child on someone's shoulders, place the carrier's hands near the child's legs and the child's reaching hand near an actual story object. A gesture aimed at empty space cannot be rescued by more foliage elsewhere.
- In an outdoor play scene, detail should explain what a structure is and how a child might use it. A plain tower or long hull reads as a cylinder or box; give a lighthouse an entrance, lookout, rails and rungs, and give a play ship a deck, portholes, rail and wheel. Plant flowers on visible rooted stems, and make a long blank path into an inlaid promenade. A distant skyline should have varied roof forms and architectural features rather than repeated cuboids. Check that added lines and flowers read as built and planted elements at portal scale, not loose polygon confetti.
- Fill branch silhouettes with several irregular 3D foliage volumes and then place individual leaves, flowers, and fruit over them. Isolated polygon leaves floating around bare twigs do not read as a full tree or a summer orchard. Let the sky have gentle pigment and cloud variation without lowering it or placing a visible roof over an outdoor memory.
- For low fruiting shrubs, small leaves also need a growth pattern: attach shaped blades to a woody stem, overlap several modest foliage volumes, and hang berry clusters by visible pedicels. Scattering petal polygons and spheres around a single round mass looks like unrelated bits at human scale. Keep the walkable lane and high open sky clear.
- A room's story figures must hold the doorway composition. In the nursing sanctuary, the first chair sat deep inside a large shell and made Jane and Zephrine tiny. Moving their entire rug, chair, table and poses forward preserved their physical relationships while making the moment visible. For a vaulted interior, use connected shaped surfaces with seams and intentional windows; separated roof rectangles and confetti on the floor look like unfinished scaffolding. Recheck collision after repositioning furniture.
- In a seated family pose, one flat sweater panel, separate tube limbs, a square side prop, and perfect concentric rug rings still read as assembled primitives even when the emotional composition works. Give the clothes continuous joints and formed knees, make side furniture specific, and let fabric edges and stitching vary. Inspect children's hair from close range after placing the painted face: added side lobes can obscure the likeness and turn into heavy bars after the whole figure is rotated.
- Design the full chair silhouette around a seated figure. A tall lofted oval behind Jane looked like a large cushion or egg; a shaped wingback crest, joined side wings and tailored piping identify it as furniture. Fewer rug contours make the textile less like a target. Do not replace a reclining child with a horizontal capsule merely because it is custom geometry: the torso, bent legs, tucked arms, face scale and actual contact with the parent must all read together in portrait phone view. Reject a custom sculpt that fails there.
- When mapping a painted likeness onto a sculpted face, compare the face's front depth with the head beneath it as well as its UV crop and color. A portrait suspended several centimeters ahead of a darker cranium reads like a pasted mask even if the source illustration is faithful. Tune the face offset and skin underpainting in one close room view, then rebuild and inspect every room sharing that person.
- In a symbolic interior, let the architecture convey the emotion. The office move had a wire fan, floating paper leaves, and many thin black shutters that obscured Jane and looked like loose construction. A joined vault, coherent floor joints, seven arched windows, and hinged partitions are more legible. Bring the story figure toward the doorway and pose her hands asymmetrically; a distant straight-armed figure cannot carry a triumphant moment.
- Check the full crown of an architectural arch against its ceiling, not just the end supports. Several gold office ribs rose through the plaster vault and appeared as disconnected halves in the painting. Lowering the apex while curving the vault kept each arch continuous. Too many bright paving lines make a symbolic floor read like a digital grid; thin them and use concrete move-specific props, such as a wheeled trolley with attached boxes, to give the space human scale.
- In an orchard, fruit and leaves need to grow from branches rather than hang near them by chance. Attach visible twigs, pedicels, and leaf clusters to the modeled limbs. A basket should have an interlaced wall, rim and paired handle, not a single brown tapered vessel. Reduce scattered floor fruit that reads as decoration; an apple as large as the child's head breaks the human scale. Check the fruit-picking moment from the doorway and among the trees.
- For a sculpted push car, ensure its identifiable features are visible from the painting camera. The first blue-car model included headlights, grille and wheels, but those parts were inset into its beveled body and vanished from the front, leaving a plain blue block. Move the modeled lamps, grille and white wheels beyond the shell, keep the dashboard cup holders and seat belt in the occupied interior, and check that hands actually meet the steering wheel and parent grip.
- On a remembered neighborhood street, repeating flat window rectangles behind a row of cloned facades still read as a stage set. Project some bays into three glazed faces with real sills and spandrels, and give entrances mounted details visible from the road. Decorative gold rings scattered on asphalt did not read as a memory of rolling wheels; use a small number of grounded street objects instead. Keep a child's scalp close behind the painted hairline, and suppress tiny shirt marks that become stray scribbles at doorway scale.
- When two modeled children hold hands, pose their actual fingers close enough to meet. A curved gold connector between distant hands reads as a rope and makes the friendship pose less believable. Give benches slats and supports rather than a single blank platform, and reduce loose petals that float apart from their trees or rooted flower stems.
- When private photos show distinctive clothing, model that garment's color blocks, trim, and drape in three dimensions. Charlize's navy striped dress with a pink waistband made her friendship-garden figure more recognizable than a generic light cone skirt. Keep a painted likeness on a curved head and fit the scalp behind it; a tall tapered hair mass or thick side curls can turn into an obvious cone or bars in the close walk-through. Ground a garden in a continuous walk and rooted stalks, and give a bridge a cambered deck and coherent rails instead of adding thin outlines around an empty slab.
- In a scene centered on a recognizable person, keep their face, clothes and hands visible in the doorway view. Frame `02`'s large blank sheet hid Oleg's torso and became the dominant primitive. Put the job-search notes on the real desk as a bound, handled object, shorten an advisor's straight arm, and give repeated versions of Jane role-specific garments or props so the acts of support differ at walking distance. Preserve the private desk photograph as reference only.
- For a walk-behind aquarium portal, inspect the rear glass from both the loop around the doorway and one step from the viewing sill. Fish distributed randomly across a tall habitat can leave the normal eye-level view almost empty, while a large animal near the pane clips across the top at close range. Compose a smaller school deeper in the water, keep its bodies at several eye-level heights, and position large species to one side. Quiet the painted far layer before adding more foreground geometry. A smooth fish body and one connected forked tail read better than angular petal fins attached to a handful of body rings.
- The mobile fish batch used to replace every mesh's vertex color with its material color, erasing any painted shading or bands. Multiply existing vertex color by material color during batching; retain the one-material export for animated schools. A texture on the source mesh alone will not survive this batching step. Inspect the exported GLB at the rear viewing sill before adding more decorative surface detail.
- A more detailed face illustration will not fix a mismatched 3D head. Oleg's alternate portrait was nearly indistinguishable in the walkable room, while the old crown and duplicate beard/sideburn lines made the face read as a mask. Fit the cranium's top and side hairline to the painted hair, remove raised lines that duplicate the texture, then inspect from under a meter away and from a three-quarter angle before rebuilding all shared appearances.
- When a face painting already contains eyeglass rims, do not trace a second 3D front frame above it. The doubled lines obscured Oleg's eyes in frame `02`; keep only the side temples needed for depth. Keep the scalp close to the painted hairline and compare the actual in-room head with the private photo before rebuilding the eight shared appearances. A new portrait must preserve his adult proportions, hair sweep, glasses, and short beard rather than simply adding pixels.
- A detailed CC0 model can replace a simplified repeated creature while still fitting the mobile budget. In frame `11`, the source fish has shaped gills, lips, fins and scales; a project-specific gouache repaint on its UV atlas keeps the fish in the room's visual language. Reduce source textures and export size, then check the fish both behind the rear glass and through the overhead pane. Model credits belong on the site credits page, and a source species is not a factual claim about the remembered visit.
- In a room surrounded by painted tank depths, carry the habitat art beyond the visible edge of the vault so a visitor behind the portal never sees a bare horizontal band. A long box sill makes the water look like a wall print; give its stone a carved silhouette and a restrained connected relief. Put some detailed, genuinely 3D animals at ordinary eye height in the side tanks as well as the focal tank, then verify their silhouettes from the room path.
- In a large rotunda, repeating perfect floor circles and several thin overhead rings can make a richly inhabited room feel like a digital target. Keep the architectural crown continuous, use a small number of irregular inlays, and spend fine geometry on carvings visibly seated in stone or other believable surfaces. Check the result from the doorway and from behind a freestanding portal; do not declare the figures finished just because the architecture improved.
- On the tall outdoor dome, the camera sees the lower half of the equirectangular atlas, not the polar cap. Place local watercolor cloud forms in that visible band and compare the exported preview before rebuilding every outdoor room. Large merged grey blooms resemble a low ceiling; smaller pale cloud forms and a stronger sky-to-horizon pigment gradient keep the world open. - Each outdoor frame is an independent sky world. Its ceiling should be an expansive sky vault beyond the reachable ground, never a small room roof.
